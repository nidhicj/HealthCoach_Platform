## 1. Purpose

After Unit_001 and Unit_002, Tapas is a strong coach-admin tool. The HC can manage clients, run sessions with AI-assisted MOM, generate diet charts, and track supplements. But the client is invisible — there is no surface where a client receives content, responds, logs meals, or pays.

This unit closes that gap. The goal: a solo HC can hand a new client the words *"Everything happens in Tapas"* and mean it — plans delivered, check-ins collected, payments settled, no WhatsApp chasing required.

**North star**: A HC running 20 clients can eliminate WhatsApp as their practice management layer.

### P0 — Must ship before any HC pilot

#### F1 — Post-session action items: HC review → freeze → notify

**One line**: After a session, the AI-drafted Session Review's action items are reviewed and finalized by the HC (never the raw review itself), then delivered to the client as a short email — just the action items plus a personal message, nothing else.

**Naming note**: "MOM"/"Session Review" (code/DB: `Mom`, `moms`) is retained as the *internal* artifact name — see Decision D-15. It is never sent to the client verbatim; see D-10 for why (it mixes client-facing content with HC-only material like next-session prep notes).

**Why P0**: The "Send" button currently changes a status flag. The client never sees anything. Every HC will ask "so how does my client get this?" and the honest answer is "WhatsApp." That's the gap this closes — deliberately with the smallest safe artifact (action items), not the full internal review.

##### HC journey

1. Coach completes session, AI drafts the Session Review (summary, discussion points, **action items**, follow-ups, closing note — existing pipeline, unchanged).
2. HC edits it in the notepad as today. The action-items sub-list stays structured and editable here (not flattened to prose) — with a nudge next to it: *"Anything listed here will be tracked and shown to your client."*
3. HC clicks **Save** (labeled "Save" in the UI, not "Freeze" — D-29; a single action, no intermediate saved-but-still-editable state). A simple yes/no confirmation appears first — *"Once saved, this locks the review and creates your client's action items — you won't be able to edit it after. Continue?"* Confirming transitions `moms.status`: `draft` → `reviewed`, locks the review text, and writes the finalized action items as real rows in `action_items` (linked via `session_id`) — all in one step. *(D-11, D-29)*
4. A dialog opens automatically: a **read-only** list of the action items just created (cannot be edited or added to here — D-12), plus an **editable** message box pre-filled from a fixed template, with a caption underneath: *"This message isn't tracked as an action item."*
5. HC can edit the message freely, clicks **Send** (`moms.status`: `reviewed` → `sent`) → email fires to the client with the action items (verbatim) + the message. *(D-13)*
6. No resend button in F1 — see D-13 for why that's an intentional, not a deferred, omission.

##### Client journey

1. Receives email from `noreply@tapas.health` (or similar) with subject along the lines of **"Your action items from [Coach name] — [Date]"**.
2. Email contains: greeting with client's first name, the finalized action items (verbatim, as a simple list with due dates where set), and the HC's personal message.
3. No account or login required to read the email (matches D-14 — F1 needs zero client-facing frontend routes; nothing here depends on the client being logged in).
4. Explicitly **not** in this email: the Session Review's summary/discussion-points/follow-ups, and the diet chart. See OQ-6 (new) for whether/how the client ever sees their diet chart at all.

##### New routes / surfaces

- No new client-facing frontend routes at all in F1 (unlike the original draft, which assumed a `/c/{token}` portal link in the email — that's gone; D-14 supersedes the token-portal model, and F1 doesn't need a portal regardless).
- HC-side: the notepad's single Save button (with its confirm dialog, D-29), the send dialog, and the "read-only action items" rendering are new frontend work on the existing session page.
- Backend: `POST /api/sessions/{id}/mom/send` is reused as a route path, but its behavior changes completely (D-9 superseded, D-13) — it now looks up `action_items` where `session_id` matches, composes the email from those + the HC's message, and transitions `reviewed` → `sent`. A new step (or a repurposed PATCH) is needed for the `draft` → `reviewed` Freeze transition, since no endpoint currently writes `action_items` rows from a Session Review.

#### F8 — Diet chart client view + version history

> **Numbering note**: this is F8, not F1b or F1.5 — appended rather than inserted, since F3–F7 already exist and are referenced elsewhere in this doc (build sequence, data model, etc.). Its **priority is P0** — it's physically placed here, right after F1, because together they're the two halves of the same gap ("client currently receives nothing"). Read priority tier, not the number, as the ordering signal throughout this doc.

**One line**: The HC's diet chart editor is unchanged and stays entirely private while being worked on. A new **"Send to client"** action snapshots the exact chart + date, which becomes what the client sees on their portal — and every past snapshot is kept, so both HC and client can look back at what the plan was on any given date across a course spanning months or years.

**Why P0**: F1 closes the "client gets nothing after a session" gap for action items. This closes the same gap for the diet chart — the other half of "everything happens in Tapas." Resolves OQ-6 (raised when F1's redesign removed the diet chart from that email).

##### HC journey

1. Diet chart editor works exactly as it does today — generate from a template + AI modifications, or hand-edit (swap a meal, adjust macros). Entirely private; the client sees none of this, no matter how many times the HC saves. *(D-16)*
2. When ready, HC clicks **Send to client**. A simple yes/no confirmation dialog appears: *"Once sent, this exact version is what your client sees, permanently. You can keep editing afterward — that becomes a new version for your next send."* No full chart re-preview in v1. *(D-18)*
3. Confirming captures a frozen snapshot (exact chart content + today's date) as a new, permanent history entry. The working chart itself is untouched and stays editable — the HC can immediately keep tweaking for next month, with none of it visible to the client until the next Send. *(D-17)*
4. HC can start a fresh AI-generation pass from any template at any point, including after a chart's already been sent — already works today with no changes needed. *(D-19)*

##### Client journey

1. Portal (once F2's client login exists) shows **the current chart** — always the latest sent snapshot, never an in-progress edit.
2. A collapsible **history** section lists every past sent version by date — each one an exact, permanently frozen copy of what was actually sent at that time.

##### New routes / surfaces

- No changes to the existing HC diet-chart editor, `generate`, or `PATCH` endpoints.
- New backend: a "Send to client" endpoint that reads the current working chart's `parameters` and inserts a snapshot row (see Data model below).
- New frontend: Send button + confirm dialog on the existing HC diet-chart editor page (`clients/[clientId]/diet-chart`).
- Client-facing display (current chart + history) is part of F2's portal work — depends on F2's login/route scheme (OQ-7), same dependency shape as F1 had before it was redesigned to need zero portal dependency. Unlike F1, this feature's *client-facing half* genuinely can't ship without F2 existing; only the HC-side send/snapshot mechanism can be built standalone.

##### Data model (sketch)

```
diet_chart_sends
  id, client_id, hc_user_id, chart_name, chart_parameters JSONB,
  sent_at TIMESTAMPTZ
```

A new, small, append-only table — deliberately decoupled from `diet_charts`/`content_assignments`, which keep serving their existing purpose (the HC's private working copy) completely unchanged.

##### 3rd party

None.

**Monthly cost**: $0.

#### F2 — Chat tab: check-ins + free messaging (redesigned 2026-07-08, D-20–D-25)

**One line**: Client detail has two tabs — Summary and Chat. Chat has three separate views a coach can flip between: Text (free messaging), Check-ins (weekly rated metrics), and Logged Meals (F3 — see its own section). This replaces the earlier 3-tab, single-merged-thread design.

**Why P0**: Action items and accountability are hollow without a client response loop. Structured check-ins give trendable data; free messaging is the in-platform replacement for the WhatsApp "quick question" habit.

##### Check-ins — the requested/answered lifecycle

- The client can already submit a check-in whenever they want, in whatever form — that keeps working exactly as it does today, untouched.
- New: the HC can request one. That request sits "waiting for an answer" until the client responds — a state that doesn't exist anywhere today, built additively on top of what's already there (D-21).
- Ten metrics, fixed by the app for now (D-22). Each week, the client picks any three of the ten and rates them — and can pick a *different* three the following week if they want. Provisional: may need to lock to a consistent three per client if pilot feedback says HCs need continuity for trend-spotting.
- Every Saturday at 9:30am, the client gets an email prompting them to fill in that week's check-in — reusing the scheduled-task mechanism already running in the backend (D-23).
- The HC never gets pinged or emailed when a check-in comes in. Their Roster Board client cards passively show what's new instead (D-24).

##### Free messaging (Text view)

- Either side can start one, any time. Async — no live/instant-delivery expectation, like email but inside the app.
- Can include a photo attachment (same private-storage approach as meal photos).
- Permanent once sent — no editing, no deleting. Kept deliberately simple (D-25).
- When the HC replies, the client gets an email. When the client sends something, the HC does **not** get emailed or pinged — same Roster Board indicator as check-ins (D-24).

##### HC journey

1. **Roster Board**: each client's card shows what's new since the HC last looked — new text, a new check-in answer, a new meal logged. No proactive ping beyond this passive indicator (D-24).
2. **Client detail → Chat tab**: flips between Text / Check-ins / Logged Meals. HC can reply to a message from the Text view, or request a check-in from the Check-ins view.
3. **Settings**: not yet designed in detail — the ten check-in metrics are app-fixed for v1 (D-22), so there's no per-coach question-authoring screen needed yet.

##### Client journey

1. Saturday 9:30am: email reminder to fill in the week's check-in.
2. Opens the app (already logged in via the existing Google sign-in — D-14), picks any three of the ten metrics, rates them, optionally adds a note.
3. Any time: opens Text, types a free message (with an optional photo), sends it.
4. When the HC replies, gets an email.

##### New routes / surfaces

- Client-facing route scheme not yet decided — see OQ-7. What *is* settled: the page structure (2 tabs on client detail; 3 views inside Chat) is independent of the exact URL naming, since the frontend currently has zero client-facing routes to migrate from.
- HC-side: Chat tab replaces the old Check-ins/Logged Meals tabs on client detail; Roster Board cards gain the new-activity indicator.
- Backend: existing check-in submission/list/flag endpoints are extended, not replaced (D-21); a new endpoint for the HC to request a check-in; new endpoints for sending/reading free messages; the Saturday reminder is a new task added to the existing scheduled-task endpoint.

##### Data model (sketch)

Existing check-in storage gets a new, nullable field marking when the HC requested one (null = today's ad hoc client-initiated behavior, unchanged; set = the new requested/answered lifecycle) — no new parallel table. A new, separate place to hold free messages (direction, body, optional attachment, sent time) — this part is genuinely new, no existing equivalent. Exact columns and indices belong in the PHASE plan, not here.

##### 3rd party

None. Built entirely in the existing Next.js + FastAPI stack, reusing the existing scheduled-task mechanism and the same private photo storage planned for meal logs.

**Monthly cost**: $0.

#### F3 — Logged Meals view (redesigned 2026-07-08, D-26)

**One line**: Clients log meals — a mandatory photo, a description, and which of five daily slots it belongs to — from the client app; the HC sees them grouped by day inside the Chat tab's Logged Meals view, and can optionally react with one of three faces. This replaces the earlier "text required, photo optional, thumbs-up only" design.

**Why P1**: Between-session meal accountability is currently on WhatsApp. This brings it into the platform with a searchable record. The HC reviews it before a session — it informs the next plan.

##### HC journey

1. **Client detail → Chat tab → Logged Meals view**: entries grouped by day, most recent day first; within a day, entries sit side by side and scroll horizontally once there are more than a few.
2. HC can optionally react to any entry with one of three faces (happy / neutral / sad) — never required, and this is the only feedback mechanic directly on the entry itself. *(D-26, supersedes D-4's single thumbs-up)*
3. If the HC wants to actually say something about a meal: that happens in the Text view, never inline on the entry — same rule as before (D-4).
4. **Roster Board**: client cards show what's new since the HC last looked, including unreacted meal logs — same passive indicator as the rest of Chat (D-24), not a push notification.

##### Client journey

1. Picks which of five slots this is: Breakfast, Morning Snack, Lunch, Evening Snack, or Dinner.
2. Attaches a photo — **mandatory**, not optional (resolves OQ-5, reversing the earlier text-only-first recommendation) — and writes a description.
3. The time shown for this entry comes from the photo's own embedded capture-time where available. What happens when that's missing, whether the client can correct it, and how to discourage picking an old gallery photo instead of shooting one live in the moment — all explicitly unresolved, deliberately pinned until the actual camera/upload flow is being built.
4. Submits → sees their own history, grouped by day like the HC's view.
5. If the HC reacts, sees the corresponding face on that entry. No email for this — reactions are a passive, in-app-only signal.

##### 3rd party

- **Photo storage**: Supabase Storage (already in stack). Private bucket, signed URLs with 1-hour expiry. Same approach reused for Text-view message attachments (F2).
- **No real-time** — async, on-demand fetch, same as the rest of Chat.

**Monthly cost**: ~$0. At 20 clients logging 2 meals/day at ~200 KB/photo: ~1.2 GB/month. Supabase free tier is 1 GB, overage is $0.021/GB — call it $0.004/month. Negligible.

#### F4 — Payment collection (architecture resolved 2026-07-08, D-27)

**One line**: Each HC connects their own Razorpay account. From a client's page, the HC sets a fee, generates a payment link, and shares it — money goes straight from client to HC, Tapas just tracks whether it's pending or paid.

**Why P1**: Solo HCs in India need to collect money before or at session start. Without this they use a separate PhonePe/UPI link + a spreadsheet. Payments in-platform is the difference between "tool I use for some things" and "my practice management system."

##### HC journey

1. **Settings**: HC connects their own Razorpay account by entering their own credentials (generated from their own Razorpay dashboard, after completing their own KYC directly with Razorpay — Tapas is never the merchant of record). Stored encrypted.
2. **Client detail → Financials section**: shows current course, fee set (₹), status (pending or paid — no partial-payment tracking in v1), due date.
3. HC enters a fee → clicks "Generate payment link" → link created and displayed.
4. HC shares the link (copy button → clipboard; WhatsApp sharing is F5 scope, post-pilot).
5. **Dashboard**: a revenue card shows this month's collected vs. outstanding across all active clients.
6. On payment: a webhook updates the status → client card shows "Paid."
7. Refunds: not built into Tapas — if an HC ever needs to refund a client, they do it directly from their own Razorpay dashboard, since the money never passed through Tapas.
8. Renewals: no automatic recurring billing — the HC generates a fresh link by hand whenever the next payment is due.

##### Client journey

1. Receives the payment link (copied and sent by the HC via whatever channel they choose — email, WhatsApp, in person).
2. Standard checkout: UPI, Google Pay, credit/debit card, net banking.
3. Receives the gateway's own standard confirmation SMS/email.
4. No change to the platform experience after payment — this is purely an HC-management feature in v1.

##### New routes / surfaces

- No new client-facing routes. Backend adds: an endpoint for the HC to connect their own gateway credentials, an endpoint to generate a payment link, and a webhook receiver for payment status updates.
- Settings page: connect payment account. Client detail page: Financials section (frontend).

##### 3rd party: Payment gateway

| Option             | Transaction fee | Setup | Notes                                                                  |
| ------------------ | --------------- | ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Razorpay** | 2% (+ GST)      | Free  | Market leader in India; UPI-first; excellent docs; webhook reliability |
| Cashfree           | 1.75%           | Free  | Slightly cheaper; growing in India                                     |
| Instamojo          | 2%              | Free  | Simpler but less feature-rich                                          |

**Recommendation**: Razorpay. Every Indian developer knows it; best documentation; most reliable webhook infrastructure; handles UPI, Google Pay, net banking, cards in one integration.

**Monthly cost**: 2% per transaction + GST (18% on the fee). No monthly fee.

Example: HC collects ₹5 000/client/month, 20 clients → ₹1 00 000 revenue. Razorpay fee: ₹2 000 + GST = ~₹2 360. Net to HC: ₹97 640.

> **Compliance note**: each HC completes their own KYC with Razorpay (business PAN, bank account) — this is between the HC and Razorpay, not something Tapas manages. Tapas stores only the reference IDs needed for tracking (order ID, payment ID, signature for verification), encrypted where they identify the HC's own account. Never store card data — Razorpay handles that entirely.
